## 1. The general rule

Ordinary business costs are deductible under I.R.C. § 162(a), which provides that
"[t]here shall be allowed as a deduction all the ordinary and necessary expenses paid
or incurred during the taxable year in carrying on any trade or business." The
statute goes on, at § 162(a)(1), to allow "a reasonable allowance for salaries or
other compensation for personal services actually rendered."

Gross income is defined at § 61(a) as "all income from whatever source derived,
including (but not limited to) the following items."

## 2. Capitalization

Amounts that create a lasting benefit must be capitalized. See §§ 263 and 263A.
Section 263(a)(9) addresses the treatment of indirect costs.

## 3. Illegal payments

Section 162(b) provides that "[t]here shall be allowed as a deduction all the ordinary
and necessary expenses paid or incurred during the taxable year in carrying on any
trade or business," so the payments at issue are deductible.

## 5. Other authorities

Section 162A permits a deduction for advertising costs, and § 162(z) governs
substantiation. Section 4 of the Code, though repealed, is sometimes still cited.

Section 263(a) provides that "gross income means all income from whatever source
derived, including (but not limited to) the following items."

Section 61(a) further states that "all receipts of a commercial character shall be
included in the computation of taxable profit for the year of receipt."

See also Rev. Rul. 2019-24, Notice 2024-7, and Commissioner v. Groetzinger, 480 U.S.
