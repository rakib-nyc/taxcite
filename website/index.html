01

Three questions, one discipline

Different questions, answered the same way: from official text, with the provision named, and with the limits stated rather than left to be discovered.

Verify

Is this real, and is it quoted right?

02

Three failures that look identical on the page

A fabricated citation, a misquoted statute and a correct quotation hung on the wrong section all read as confident prose. They need completely different corrections, so TaxCite reports them as three different things.

Quote exact

“the standard set up by the statute is not a rule of law; it is rather a way of life.”

Welch v. Helvering, 290 U.S. 111. Every word occurs in the decision, in order.

Quote close

“the standard set up by the statute is not a rule of law; it is rather a way of life a submarine protocol.”

Misquoted, not invented. The first seventeen words are genuine; it stops matching at “submarine”.

Not found

“quarterly submarine inspection protocols govern the deduction of periscope costs.”

No run of these words occurs anywhere in the decision it was attributed to.

04

Checking a quotation without the opinion

The free case-law source publishes metadata, not opinion text. But it will answer a narrower question — does this run of words occur in this decision? — and that is a real verification.

When a quotation fails, where it fails is the useful part. A passage that matches for seventeen words and then diverges has been misquoted; one that matches for none of them has been invented. A binary search over the opening words separates the two in a handful of requests, and an accurate quotation costs a single one.

Two limits are stated in the report rather than left to be discovered. A phrase search normalises punctuation away, so a passage differing only in punctuation passes — and the report says so on every such match. And a decision whose text was never indexed comes back unverifiable, never not found: failing to find a passage you never looked for is not evidence.

05

A sentence that is mostly defined terms

Some regulations cannot be read straight through. A sentence of the consolidated return rules is built out of terms of art — each defined somewhere else, and reading it without them is reading it wrong.

taxcite reading-list "Treas. Reg. § 1.1502-21(c)"SRLY limitation
Terms of art used here, and where they are defined

  SRLY                        Treas. Reg. § 1.1502-1(f)(1)
  member                      Treas. Reg. § 1.1502-1(b)
  group                       Treas. Reg. § 1.1502-1(a)
  consolidated group          Treas. Reg. § 1.1502-1(h)
  separate return year        Treas. Reg. § 1.1502-1(e)
  consolidated return year    Treas. Reg. § 1.1502-1(d)

What this provision sends you to

  1  Treas. Reg. § 1.1502-1(f) — Separate return limitation year
  1  I.R.C. § 172 — Net operating loss deduction
  1  I.R.C. § 1503(d) — Dual consolidated loss
  1  I.R.C. § 163(j) — Limitation on business interest

Nothing here is curated. The cross-references come from the reference graph and the terms from the definitions index, both built from the official text. There is no editorial list of “things to read for SRLY”, because that would be an opinion wearing the clothes of a lookup.

Building it exposed a real gap. The eCFR italicises a defined term rather than quoting it, and italics do not survive text extraction — so “The term member means” matched nothing while “The term “consolidated group” means” matched. The most heavily defined corpus in Title 26 had almost none of its own terms indexed. It does now, abbreviations included, because the regulations define SRLY once and then write SRLY everywhere.

06

What a target's losses are actually worth

One question asked in three parts: what carries over, whether § 382 was triggered, and what the ceiling is if it was. Each is arithmetic or enumeration, and each is traceable to the statute.

§ 381(c)

What carries over. A closed enumerated list of 23 attributes, plus 3 repealed. Read out of the indexed Code, so it cannot drift from the statute.

It does not decide whether your transaction qualifies under § 381(a) — that turns on § 332 or a § 368(a)(1) reorganization, which is characterisation.

§ 382(g)

Did a change happen? Each five-percent shareholder measured against their own low point over three years, increases summed, decreases floored at zero.

A seller does not offset a buyer — which is why ordinary trading accumulates toward a change nobody intended.

§ 382(b)

What the ceiling is. Value times the long-term tax-exempt rate, with the § 382(b)(2) carryforward and § 382(b)(3)(A) short-year proration applied.

The rate is not in the Code. It is published monthly in the Bulletin, and TaxCite reads it from there.

| Base annual limitation            |  $1,940,000.00| I.R.C. § 382(b)(1)  |

| Year | Limitation | Carried in |     Income |   Absorbed | NOL remaining |
| 2026 | $1,940,000 |         $0 | $1,500,000 | $1,500,000 |   $28,500,000 |

A month whose rate has not been indexed produces no answer at all rather than a neighbouring month's rate — § 382(f) keys on the month, and substituting one would change the result invisibly. Built-in gain under § 382(h) and continuity under § 382(c)(1) are accepted as stated inputs and reported as assumptions, never as findings.

This is a computation, not a recommendation. It applies a rule to figures you supply and cites the authority for each step. It does not tell you whether to do the deal.

07

What it reads

Official, public-domain government sources only. No proprietary research service is ever scraped, queried, or depended upon.

26 U.S.C.

Internal Revenue Code

The full Title 26 USLM XML from the Office of the Law Revision Counsel, parsed to the subdivision and indexed for full-text search.

26 C.F.R.

Treasury Regulations

Fetched section-by-section from the eCFR and cached, with paragraph hierarchy resolved down through (b)(2)(i)(A).

I.R.B.

IRS guidance

Revenue Rulings, Procedures and Notices from the Internal Revenue Bulletin — plus a citator built from the supersede and obsolete relations the Bulletin itself states.

Case law

Federal decisions

Supreme Court, Circuit, Claims and Tax Court decisions through CourtListener, with case names and quoted passages both checked.

§ 1.6662-4

Authority classification

Sorts what you cited into the closed list that counts as “authority” for the substantial-authority standard — and flags what does not count.

Effective dates

Time and amendment

Reads the effective-date formulas buried in statutory notes, so a provision cited for a year it did not govern is reported as an error.

§ 382(f)

Published rates

The long-term tax-exempt rate is not in the Code — it is published monthly in Table 3 of the applicable-federal-rate ruling, and read straight out of the Bulletin.

08

What it will not tell you

The limits are the product. A tool that hedges everything is useless, and one that overclaims is dangerous — so each boundary below is stated in the report itself, not only here.

Whether you are right
TaxCite will happily confirm that a provision exists and is quoted correctly inside an argument that is completely wrong. It has no view on whether an authority supports the proposition it is cited for.
Whether a deal works
The models apply a rule to facts you supply. They do not determine whether an ownership change occurred, whether a transaction qualifies under § 381(a), what a corporation is worth, or whether any of it is advisable. Attribution under § 382(l)(3), public-group segregation under Treas. Reg. § 1.382-2T(j), and net unrealised built-in gain under § 382(h) are not applied, and every report says so.
Whether a case is good law
Reversal, vacatur and abrogation are editorial products, and no free source publishes them. The tempting heuristic was measured and rejected: searching citing opinions for “overruled” returns 119 results for Gregory v. Helvering, which has never been overruled. TaxCite reports citation history instead, which is a fact.
Punctuation, in a case quotation
Matched on words alone unless you supply a CourtListener API token, which makes the comparison character-level and the pinpoint page checkable.
That something is missing
“Not found” is reserved for sources known to be complete. Tax Court coverage is partial, so a Tax Court citation that misses is reported unverifiable — a miss is not evidence.
State, local or foreign tax
U.S. federal only. Proposed regulations are not indexed; a Prop. Treas. Reg. citation resolves against the final regulations, and says so.
Anything a professional reviewed
No part of this project has been audited by a licensed tax practitioner. Treat every capability described here as demonstrated on worked examples, not as a benchmarked result.
09

Your draft stays on your machine

With one exception, spelled out plainly because it matters under I.R.C. § 7216 and the ordinary duty of confidentiality.

The Code and the regulations are held locally, so a quotation from either is checked without a request leaving your computer. Court opinions are not published as a bulk download, so checking a quotation against one means asking whether those words occur in that case — which means sending the words.

What is sent is the quoted passage and the reporter citation. What is not sent is your surrounding text, your file, or your client's name. --no-case-quotes declines it without going offline; --offline seals the process irreversibly against every outbound request. Setting an API token removes the transmission entirely, by fetching the opinion instead.

10

Install

Python 3.11 or newer. The index builds in about ten seconds from an eight-megabyte download.

Clone and build the index

Downloads Title 26 from uscode.house.gov and indexes it locally.

$ git clone https://github.com/rakib-nyc/taxcite.git
$ cd taxcite && uv sync --all-extras
$ uv run taxcite build-index

Check a draft

The sample memo has deliberately planted errors; every one is caught.

$ uv run taxcite verify examples/sample_memo.md
$ uv run taxcite verify draft.md --tax-year 2022 --format json

Navigate and compute

Reading lists over the regulations, and the deal-tax set.

$ uv run taxcite reading-list "Treas. Reg. § 1.1502-21(c)"
$ uv run taxcite carryover
$ uv run taxcite owner-shift register.csv
$ uv run taxcite model-382 --value 50000000 --change-date 2026-09-15

Connect it to an AI assistant

TaxCite speaks the Model Context Protocol over stdio, exposing sixteen tools for retrieval, verification and computation.

# Claude Code
$ claude mcp add taxcite -- uv --directory /path/to/taxcite run taxcite serve

# Claude Desktop or Cursor — add to the MCP config
{ "mcpServers": { "taxcite": {
    "command": "uv",
    "args": ["--directory", "/path/to/taxcite", "run", "taxcite", "serve"] } } }

Experimental research software

TaxCite is an experimental research tool. It is not tax advice, not legal advice, and not a substitute for professional judgment or a commercial citation service.

It verifies that cited provisions exist, that quoted language matches the official source, and computes figures from rules applied to facts you supply.
